\chapter*{Abstract}

Mutual information (MI) measures the average reduction in uncertainty about
one variable obtained by observing another. This thesis develops and
evaluates a test for comparing MI between two categorical variables using
independent samples from two populations. The Wald test uses a standard
normal reference distribution and can give inaccurate false-positive rates
when samples are small or many category combinations are rarely observed.

The proposed Expanded Welch--Satterthwaite test (Expanded Welch) retains
Wald's estimated MI difference and standard error, but uses a Student
\(t\) distribution to decide whether the difference is statistically
significant. Its degrees of freedom reflect how much the variance estimates
underlying the standard error vary between samples relative to their
magnitudes. We derive a first-order approximation to this sampling variation
and use it to calculate the degrees of freedom, giving a larger rejection
cutoff than Wald's.

Simulations vary sample size, table size and category frequencies under
equal and unequal population MI, while an online-shopping example
illustrates the tests on observed data. Expanded Welch brings some excessive
false-positive rates closer to the 5\% target, but lowers them further
where Wald already rejects too rarely.
Its larger cutoff also reduces power, the probability of detecting a real
MI difference, although the power difference between methods becomes small
with larger samples. When the two variables are nearly independent,
sampling noise creates apparent association in their observed tables, and
the first-order approximation can miss substantial sampling variation.
In sparse settings, bias can even make the estimated difference point to
the wrong population as having greater MI. The larger cutoff therefore
improves some false-positive rates, but cannot correct bias in the MI
difference or errors in its estimated standard error.
