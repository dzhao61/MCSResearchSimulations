\chapter*{Abstract}

For two categorical variables, mutual information (MI) measures how much
observing one reduces uncertainty about the other, on average. Independent
samples from two populations can give different MI estimates even when the
populations have equal MI. With small samples or rarely observed category
combinations, the Wald test can reject equal MI too often or too rarely.

This thesis develops the Expanded Welch--Satterthwaite test (Expanded Welch)
for comparing MI between two populations. It retains the estimated MI
difference and standard error used by the Wald test but replaces the standard
normal reference distribution with a Student \(t\) distribution.
The standard error depends on variance estimates that change between
samples. We derive a first-order approximation to how much these estimates
vary and use it to estimate the degrees of freedom. The resulting Student
\(t\) distribution gives the Expanded Welch test a larger rejection cutoff
than that of the Wald test.
Simulations under equal and
unequal population MI assess approximation accuracy, false-positive rates
and power across sample sizes, numbers of categories and category
frequencies. An online-shopping example illustrates the tests on observed
data.

In the simulations, the Expanded Welch test brings some excessive false-positive
rates closer to the 5\% target, but lowers rejection further where the Wald test
already rejects too rarely. The larger cutoff can reduce power, the
probability of detecting a real MI difference, although the power difference
between methods becomes small with larger samples. The first-order
approximation also becomes less accurate when the variables are nearly
independent. In sparse settings, bias can reverse which population
appears to have greater MI. The larger cutoff therefore improves some
false-positive rates but cannot correct bias in the estimated MI difference
or errors in its standard error.
