\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Mutual information (MI) measures how much observing one variable reduces
uncertainty about another. This thesis develops and evaluates a test for
equal MI between two categorical variables across independent populations,
allowing their probability tables to differ. The Normal Wald test uses a
normal approximation for this comparison, which can give inaccurate
false-positive rates in small samples or when many category combinations
are rarely observed.

The proposed Expanded Welch--Satterthwaite test (Expanded Welch) retains
Wald's estimated MI difference and standard error but compares the shared
statistic with a Student \(t\) distribution. To choose its degrees of
freedom, we derive a first-order approximation to how much each variance
estimate used in the standard error changes between samples. The
calculation tracks an observation's contribution to that estimate,
including changes in cell probabilities, their row and column totals,
and pointwise MI.

Simulations from known populations assess these approximations and compare
false-positive rates and power to detect genuine MI differences, while an
online-shopping application illustrates the tests on observed data. Near
independence, apparent association from sampling noise can make the
degrees-of-freedom approximations inaccurate. Expanded Welch brings
false-positive rates closer to 5\% in selected small-sample settings,
with lower power at the usual rejection thresholds, but also reduces
rejection where false-positive rates are already below 5\%.

To compare power after matching false-positive rates, we use independent
equal-MI simulations to fit separate rejection thresholds targeting 5\%
false positives at a chosen common MI value. Expanded Welch then has higher
power in some comparisons, but these gains can reverse when the common MI
used for fitting changes. Changing thresholds leaves bias in the MI
difference and errors in its estimated standard error unresolved. The
evidence therefore supports improvements in particular tested settings,
with power gains that depend on the calibration population.
