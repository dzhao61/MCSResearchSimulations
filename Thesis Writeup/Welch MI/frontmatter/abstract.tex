\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Mutual information (MI) measures association between two variables. This
thesis asks whether two independently sampled populations have equal MI,
even when their joint probability tables differ. The usual Normal Wald test
uses a normal approximation that can be inaccurate in small or sparse
samples.

The thesis develops an Expanded Welch--Satterthwaite adjustment for this
comparison. It derives how changing one observation affects the estimated MI
variance, including changes to the cell probabilities and their row and
column totals. That calculation determines the degrees of freedom for a
Student \(t\) distribution. Expanded Welch retains Wald's estimated MI
difference and standard error; it changes only the distribution used to judge
the resulting statistic.

The methods are evaluated in 3,111 fixed statistical settings, each with
20,000 independently sampled table pairs. The design includes square tables
from \(2\times2\) to \(8\times8\), selected rectangular tables, unequal
sample sizes, sparse cells, and populations with different distributions but
equal MI. At a nominal 5\% threshold, the experiment measures false
positives, detection when MI differs, and the frequency of usable results.

Expanded Welch reduces excessive Wald false positives in some settings, but
the same adjustment also reduces rejection elsewhere. When both tests return
a p-value, its Student \(t\) distribution gives a p-value no smaller than
Wald's. It therefore lowers rejection where Wald
already rejects too rarely and can reduce detection when MI differs. In
sparse samples, Expanded Welch more often fails to return a usable p-value
under the implemented rule. Wald is closer to the target 5\% rate in all 24
main null settings with sample sizes 500 or 1,000.

Changing the reference distribution leaves errors in the shared standard
error unchanged, as illustrated by a supplementary diagnostic.
For the settings studied, Wald remains
the simpler general baseline. Expanded Welch is a potential additional check
when a separate, closely matched calibration study shows that Wald rejects
equal MI too often.
