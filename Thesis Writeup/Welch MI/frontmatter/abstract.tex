\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Mutual information (MI) measures dependence between two variables. This
thesis tests whether two independent categorical populations have equal MI,
without requiring their joint distributions or margins to be the same.

Normal Wald inference standardises a bias-corrected MI difference using a
plug-in standard error and a normal reference. Expanded Welch--Satterthwaite
keeps that statistic but derives effective degrees of freedom from the
sampling variability of the estimated MI variance. The derivation includes
changes to the cell probabilities, margins and pointwise MI values. Its
Student reference is a moment-matching approximation, not an exact
finite-sample distribution.

The experiment covers fixed positive-support populations from \(2\times2\)
to \(8\times8\), with rectangular, unequal-sample, construction and
extreme-sparsity checks. Each of 3,111 configurations uses 20,000 paired
replicates at a two-sided nominal level of 0.05. False positives, detection,
validity and runtime are reported by regime. The null cases distinguish
identical populations from distinct populations with equal MI.

Expanded Welch improves some liberal Wald false-positive rates but worsens
already conservative regimes. Its strict component-validity rule also
produces fewer usable results in sparse samples. Wald is closer to nominal
in all 24 main null regimes at sample sizes 500 or 1,000. At common MI 0.02
and sample size 50,000, both methods are near nominal in the tested
populations; very small MI remains difficult. Expanded Welch has the same
computational order and costs about 1.75 times as much per scalar call in the
measured implementation.

Near independence, small effective degrees of freedom make Expanded Welch
strongly conservative. A supplementary diagnostic shows why changing only
the reference cannot correct errors in both the numerator and standard error.

The evidence supports Wald as the simpler general baseline, with Expanded
Welch as a sensitivity analysis in selected liberal regimes.
