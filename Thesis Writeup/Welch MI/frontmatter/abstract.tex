\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Mutual information (MI) measures how much observing one variable reduces
uncertainty about another. This thesis develops and evaluates a test for
equal MI between two categorical variables across independent populations.
The Normal Wald test uses a normal approximation for this comparison,
which can give inaccurate
false-positive rates in small samples or when many category combinations
are rarely observed.

The proposed Expanded Welch--Satterthwaite test (Expanded Welch) retains
Wald's estimated MI difference and standard error but compares the shared
statistic with a Student \(t\) distribution. To choose its degrees of
freedom, we derive a first-order approximation to how much each estimated
pointwise-MI variance changes between samples. The
calculation tracks an observation's contribution to that estimate,
including changes in cell probabilities, their row and column totals,
and pointwise MI.

Simulations from known populations assess these approximations and compare
false-positive rates across three common MI values and power across three
MI-gap magnitudes, while an online-shopping application illustrates the
tests on observed data. Near
independence, apparent association from sampling noise can make the
degrees-of-freedom approximations inaccurate. Expanded Welch's larger
cutoff brings some excessive false-positive rates closer to 5\%, but
also increases conservatism where Wald already rejects too rarely and
reduces power at the usual rejection thresholds. The power difference
between methods becomes small with larger samples, while smaller MI
gaps remain harder to detect once bias diminishes. Changing the cutoff
leaves bias in the shared MI difference and errors in its estimated
standard error unchanged. In sparse settings, bias in the MI difference
can also produce rejection in the opposite direction to the true difference.
