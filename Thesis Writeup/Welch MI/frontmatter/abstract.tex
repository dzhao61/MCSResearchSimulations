\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Mutual information (MI) measures how much knowing one variable reduces
uncertainty about another. This thesis develops a test for comparing MI
between two categorical variables across independent populations. The
Normal Wald test uses a normal approximation for this comparison, which
can give inaccurate false-positive rates when samples are small or contain
many rare category combinations.

The proposed Expanded Welch--Satterthwaite test (Expanded Welch) retains
Wald's estimated MI difference and standard error but compares the test
statistic with a Student \(t\) distribution. To choose its degrees of
freedom, the thesis derives a first-order approximation to how much the
variance estimate within that standard error changes between samples.
The calculation accounts for changes in cell probabilities and their row
and column totals.

Simulations assess the approximations and compare false-positive rates
and power to detect genuine MI differences. Near independence, sampling
noise can make the degrees-of-freedom approximations inaccurate. An
online-shopping application illustrates the tests on observed data.

Expanded Welch brings false-positive rates closer to 5\% in selected
small-sample settings, with lower power under the usual rejection
thresholds. We also adjust the rejection thresholds to give both tests
approximately 5\% false positives at a chosen common MI value. Expanded
Welch then has higher power in some comparisons, but this advantage can
reverse when a different common MI value is used for the adjustment.
Changing the rejection threshold cannot correct bias in the MI difference
or errors in its estimated uncertainty.
