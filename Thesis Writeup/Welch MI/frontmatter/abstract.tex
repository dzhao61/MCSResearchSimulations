\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Mutual information (MI) measures the average reduction in uncertainty about
one variable obtained by observing another.
This thesis compares MI between the same two categorical variables in
two independently sampled populations whose joint probability tables
can differ even when their MI values are equal. The Normal Wald test uses a normal
approximation that can be inaccurate in small or sparse samples.

The thesis develops an Expanded Welch--Satterthwaite test by
deriving how changing one observation affects the estimated MI variance.
By accounting for changes to cell probabilities and their row and
column totals, the calculation supplies the degrees of freedom for a
Student \(t\) distribution while retaining Wald's estimated difference
and standard error.

Three controlled studies examine false positives, degrees of freedom and
statistical power to detect MI differences across \(2\times2,4\times4,8\times8\) and
\(16\times16\) tables and three margin regimes. Supporting checks
vary construction and sample allocation. Each of the 240 settings uses
20,000 sampled table pairs. Diagnostics assess the shared MI estimates
and the predicted sampling variability of the estimated MI variance.

Expanded Welch brings false-positive rates closer to 5\% in some small-sample
settings, but makes already-conservative rates lower still. Near independence,
small estimated degrees of freedom reinforce this conservatism. In
\(16\times16\) tables with different marginal probabilities at
\(n=1{,}000\), unequal MI bias shifts the estimated difference away
from zero. False-positive rates are
0.2522 for Wald and 0.2402 for Expanded Welch, with every calculation
valid. At small nonzero differences, this bias can also reverse the
estimated ordering among rejections. The supporting construction check
reproduces both failures. Larger samples substantially reduce
bias, improve power and bring the two methods closer together.

The evaluation does not support Expanded Welch as a general replacement
for Normal Wald, because its larger rejection cutoff cannot correct
errors in the shared MI difference or standard error.
