\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Mutual information (MI) measures the average reduction in uncertainty about
one variable obtained by observing another.
This thesis compares MI between the same two categorical variables in
two independently sampled populations. Their joint probability tables
can differ even when their MI values are equal. Normal Wald uses a normal
approximation that can be inaccurate in small or sparse samples.

The thesis develops an Expanded Welch--Satterthwaite test by
deriving how changing one observation affects the estimated MI variance.
The calculation includes changes to cell probabilities and their row
and column totals. It supplies the degrees of freedom for a Student
\(t\) distribution, while retaining Wald's estimated difference and
standard error.

Three controlled studies examine false positives, degrees of freedom across
population MI values, and detection across \(2\times2,4\times4,8\times8\) and
\(16\times16\) tables and three margin regimes. Supporting checks
vary construction and sample allocation. Each of the 240 settings uses
20,000 sampled table pairs. Diagnostics assess the shared MI estimates
and the predicted sampling variability of the estimated MI variance.

Expanded Welch's larger cutoff lowers rejection, helping where Wald rejects
equal MI too often but making conservative rates lower still. Near independence,
small estimated degrees of freedom reinforce this conservatism. In
\(16\times16\) different-skew copying tables at \(n=1{,}000\), unequal MI
bias shifts the estimated difference away from zero: false-positive
rates are 0.2522 for Wald and 0.2402 for Expanded Welch despite full
validity. At small nonzero differences, this bias can also reverse the
estimated direction among rejections. Excessive false positives and reversed
ordering also occur under the matched four-cell construction.
At \(n=10{,}000\), bias is much smaller under both constructions.
Larger samples also improve detection and reduce the difference between methods.

The evaluation does not support Expanded Welch as a general replacement
for Normal Wald. Its larger rejection cutoff cannot correct errors in the
shared MI difference or standard error.
