\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Mutual information (MI) measures association between two variables.
This thesis compares the MI of two independently sampled categorical
populations, allowing their joint probability tables to differ even
when their MI values are equal. The Normal Wald test uses a normal
approximation that can be inaccurate in small or sparse samples.

The thesis develops an Expanded Welch--Satterthwaite adjustment by
deriving how changing one observation affects the estimated MI variance.
The calculation includes changes to cell probabilities and their row
and column totals. It supplies the degrees of freedom for a Student
\(t\) distribution, while retaining Wald's estimated difference and
standard error.

Three controlled studies examine false positives, association strength
and detection. The design uses \(2\times2,4\times4,8\times8\) and
\(16\times16\) probability tables, three margin regimes and equal
sample sizes, with 20,000 sampled table pairs in each of 232 settings.
Bias and variance diagnostics explain the observed patterns, and a
small alternative-construction check assesses a representative comparison.

Expanded Welch lowers rejection, helping where Wald rejects equal MI
too often but making conservative rates lower still. Near independence,
small estimated degrees of freedom reinforce this conservatism. In
\(16\times16\) different-skew tables at \(n=1{,}000\), unequal MI
bias shifts the estimated difference away from zero: false-positive
rates are 0.2522 for Wald and 0.2402 for Expanded Welch despite full
validity. At \(n=10{,}000\), the bias is much smaller and the rates
are 0.0463 and 0.0453. Larger samples also improve detection and reduce
the difference between methods.

Changing the reference distribution cannot correct errors in the shared
MI difference or standard error. Normal Wald remains the simpler baseline
for this task, with Expanded Welch a potential supplementary check where
a closely matched calibration study supports its use.
