\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Mutual information (MI) measures the average reduction in uncertainty about
one variable obtained by observing another. This thesis develops and
evaluates a test for comparing MI between two categorical variables using
independent samples from two populations. The Normal Wald test can give
inaccurate false-positive rates when samples are small or many category
combinations are rarely observed.

The proposed Expanded Welch--Satterthwaite test (Expanded Welch) retains
Wald's estimated MI difference and standard error, but uses a Student
\(t\) distribution to decide whether the difference is statistically
significant. We derive a first-order approximation to how the variance
estimates underlying the standard error change between samples and use it
to calculate the distribution's degrees of freedom. The resulting
rejection cutoff is larger than Wald's.

Simulations vary sample size, table size and category frequencies under
equal and unequal population MI, while an online-shopping example
illustrates the tests on observed data. These simulations show that, when
the two variables are nearly independent, sampling noise creates apparent
association in their observed tables, and the first-order approximation can
miss substantial sampling variation.
Expanded Welch brings some excessive false-positive rates closer to the
5\% target, but lowers them further where Wald already rejects too rarely.
Its larger cutoff can also reduce power, the probability of detecting a real
MI difference, although the power difference between methods becomes small
with larger samples. Changing the cutoff leaves bias in the shared MI
difference and errors in its estimated standard error unchanged. In sparse
settings, this remaining bias can even make the estimated difference point
to the wrong population as having greater MI.
