The smaller tables again give low false-positive rates at intermediate
sample sizes. At \(n=100\), Wald rejects at 0.0128 under uniform
margins and 0.0114 under same skew, with still lower Expanded Welch
rates. In \(8\times8\) tables, uniform margins instead give rates
0.0737 and 0.0651 at this size, whereas same-skew margins give 0.0189
and 0.0121. The effect of the margin regime therefore changes with
table size. By \(n=50{,}000\), all four comparisons have rates
between 0.0489 and 0.0503 and full validity.

\section{Supporting estimation diagnostics}

Table~\ref{tab:association-diagnostics} gives the diagnostics behind
the association-strength figure. Est./obs. is the ratio of mean squared
standard error to observed sampling variance. Pop./obs. replaces the
estimated variance with the population first-order variance
\(V(P)/n+V(Q)/n\). DF denotes combined degrees of freedom; its bracketed
range gives the 5th and 95th percentiles. All repetitions have finite
diagnostics and valid test outputs in these settings.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\caption{Diagnostics for \(4\times4\) different-skew populations with equal MI.}
\label{tab:association-diagnostics}
\input{figures_rewrite/focused_association_table}
\end{table}

At the smallest MI targets, population first-order variance accounts
for only a small fraction of the observed spread at either sample size.
Meanwhile, the plug-in variance estimate exceeds that spread. Using
estimated rather than population inputs therefore changes the error
as well as its size. Both comparisons become much closer to one as
association strengthens, especially at \(n=1{,}000\). This supports
reading the low rejection rates together with estimation error, rather
than attributing them solely to the Student \(t\) distribution.

Table~\ref{tab:large-sample-diagnostics} continues the main diagnostic
comparison to \(n=50{,}000\) at common MI 0.02. All outputs are valid.
At \(4\times4\) the difference bias has Monte Carlo standard error
approximately 0.000010 nats; at \(16\times16\) it is approximately
0.000012. The biases are small, the estimated/observed variance ratios
are near one, and the large degrees of freedom bring the two reference
distributions close together. The remaining population/observed
discrepancy at \(16\times16\) is larger than that at \(4\times4\).

\section{Detection over the full difference range}

Figures~\ref{fig:focused-detection-full} and
\ref{fig:focused-margin-detection-full} show the same settings as the
main detection figures, extending the horizontal range through 0.2 nats.
They retain the zero-difference points and Wilson interval bands.
The population targets are \(I(P)=0.02\) and
\(I(Q)=0.02+|\Delta|\); every result is valid in these settings.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics{focused_detection_sizes_full.pdf}
\caption{Full-range detection under different-skew margins.}
\label{fig:focused-detection-full}
\end{figure}

At \(n=1{,}000\), detection of a 0.05-nat difference falls with table
size: Wald rates are 0.9642, 0.9079, 0.7699 and 0.0471 from
\(2\times2\) through \(16\times16\). The last curve first falls
from its high null rate, then rises to 0.6297 at 0.1 nats and 0.9997
at 0.2. Unequal bias opposes the small negative true differences there,
as discussed in the main results. At \(n=10{,}000\), both methods
detect every simulated 0.05-nat difference at all four sizes.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics{focused_detection_margins_full.pdf}
\caption{Full-range detection across \(4\times4\) margin regimes.}
\label{fig:focused-margin-detection-full}
\end{figure}

The margin regimes differ most at intermediate effects, before rejection
approaches one. At 0.2 nats, both methods reject every simulated sample
at both sample sizes in all three regimes. Near this upper limit,
a smaller difference between methods says little about their relative
performance for weaker effects; the enlarged main figures show those
effects more clearly.

\section{Four-cell construction check}

Table~\ref{tab:construction-check} compares the copying and four-cell
populations at matched \(4\times4\) different-skew margins and MI
targets. All outputs and diagnostics are finite and valid. The sampled
populations differ between constructions, so the cross-construction
comparison uses independent repetitions. Within each construction,
Wald and Expanded Welch receive the same table pairs.

The same qualitative method comparison persists: at \(n=1{,}000\),
Expanded Welch lowers an already-low null rate and reduces detection;
at 10,000 its effect is smaller. The construction still changes the
numerical detection rate. For the 0.01-nat difference at \(n=10{,}000\),
Wald detects at 0.7962 under copying and 0.7865 under the four-cell
transfer. The difference, 0.0098, has approximate independent-stream
95\% interval [0.0018, 0.0177]. Thus the check supports the direction
of the method comparison at these settings, not identical performance
under every association pattern.
