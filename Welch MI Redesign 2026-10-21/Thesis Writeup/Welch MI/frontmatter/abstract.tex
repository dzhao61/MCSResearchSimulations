\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Mutual information (MI) measures how much observing one variable reduces
uncertainty about another. This thesis considers whether MI between two
categorical variables differs across independent populations. A Normal
Wald test provides one approach, but small or sparse samples can distort
the estimated difference and its standard error, producing unreliable
false-positive rates and power.

This thesis develops an Expanded Welch--Satterthwaite test that retains
Wald's estimated difference and standard error and uses a Student \(t\)
distribution. Its degrees of freedom depend on how much the estimated
MI variance changes between samples. The thesis derives a first-order
approximation to that variability by accounting for changes in cell
probabilities, their margins and the resulting MI values.

Simulations first check the moment approximations used in the derivation.
They then compare false-positive rates and power with Normal Wald and
a sample-size degrees-of-freedom rule. Independently fitted cutoffs allow
a further power comparison at approximately equal false-positive rates.
An online-shopping dataset illustrates interpretation on observed data.

The mean and sampling variance approximations can have substantially
different errors. Expanded Welch improves calibration in selected
small-sample populations and can improve power at matched false-positive
rates. However, changing the cutoff does not correct sparse MI bias or
inaccurate uncertainty estimates. The results identify where MI-specific
degrees of freedom are useful and the estimation problems that limit
their application.
